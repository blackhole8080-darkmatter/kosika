The Fibonacci sequence, in a real sunflower head

Its seeds sit in two sets of spirals whose counts are neighbouring Fibonacci numbers. Hover a sentence to see it on the photo.

centre x 353 y 223 disc r 140 px

1Each new seed grows at the centre, turned 137.5° from the last: the golden angle.

2No seed sits behind another, so they pack tight and line up in spirals: about 21 turn clockwise...

3...and 34 turn the other way.

421 and 34 are neighbours in the Fibonacci sequence, where each number is the sum of the two before it.

5Kosika counted 23 and 31 on this very photo, from its centre at (353, 223).

Measured: 23 clockwise and 31 counter-clockwise spirals, within 12% of 21 and 34, neighbours in the Fibonacci sequence. A vision model (qwen/qwen3.8-27b) first guessed the centre at (320, 240) and 34 / 55 spirals; Kosika measured its own.

Also suggested: Nautilus shell, Romanesco broccoli. Kosika can measure only sunflower heads on a photo so far.
